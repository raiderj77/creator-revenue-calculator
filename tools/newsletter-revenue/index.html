How the newsletter scenario works

Every result is traceable to a visible field.

Subscription formula

Paid subscribers are either entered directly or calculated from list size multiplied by your conversion assumption. Gross subscription revenue equals paid subscribers multiplied by monthly price. The platform fee is the gross amount multiplied by the percentage you enter.

Completed-work rule

Sponsorship and referral lines use completed or confirmed counts multiplied by the net amount entered. This avoids turning an unbooked offer, public CPM claim, or unapproved referral into revenue.

Costs and annualization

Other monthly costs are subtracted once. The annual figure simply multiplies the monthly scenario by twelve; it does not model churn, growth, seasonality, taxes, currency conversion, refunds not entered, or changing platform terms.

Records to retain

  • The reporting period and paid-subscriber count.
  • The billing record or agreement supporting the platform-fee input.
  • Completed sponsorship invoices and confirmed referral reports.
  • Processing, software, refund, contractor, and operating costs included in the scenario.

Substack fees: map each charge once

Substack’s cost documentation lists a 10% platform fee, US card processing of 2.9% + $0.30 per payment, and a 0.7% Stripe Billing fee on recurring payments, including non-card methods. Other payment methods and countries have different processing charges.

For that US card scenario, enter 13.6 in the percentage-fee field (10 + 2.9 + 0.7). Enter $0.30 multiplied by the number of payments in other monthly costs, alongside software and other costs. Do not also enter the percentage charges in monthly costs. Alternatively, use 0% and enter the actual total fees from your payout ledger in monthly costs. Annual payments require their actual payment count; do not charge the fixed fee monthly when no payment occurred.

Stripe Tax Basic pricing separately lists 0.5% per transaction where registered to collect tax for its no-code integration. Include it only if your account actually incurs that fee; it is not an automatic charge for every Substack writer. Tax Complete and API pricing differ.

Synthetic example: 100 monthly $5 payments produce $500 gross. Percentage fees at 13.6% are $68; fixed charges are $30. That leaves $402 before any other costs or taxes. These invented inputs demonstrate arithmetic, not expected earnings.
