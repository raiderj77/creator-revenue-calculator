# Editorial style guide

## Structure

- Begin with the direct answer, decision, or workflow.
- Use a descriptive H1 and short sections with informative headings.
- Explain the related calculator or tracker before adding supporting detail.
- End with limitations, primary sources, and one relevant next action.
- There is no required word count, section count, keyword density, CTA quota, or publication cadence.

## Evidence

- Cite exact platform fees, eligibility criteria, policy statements, and definitions to current primary sources.
- Keep source URLs, source update dates when available, date checked, and next review date in the publication registry.
- Label illustrative numbers as a “synthetic example” and list every input.
- Do not present a synthetic example as typical, observed, or predictive.
- Avoid unsourced superlatives, market-size claims, income ranges, and conversion assumptions.

## Terminology

- Prefer `estimate`, `scenario`, `input`, `assumption`, `gross`, `deduction`, and `net`.
- Avoid `guaranteed`, `expected earnings`, `typical creator`, `industry standard`, and `passive income` unless a primary source and precise context make the phrase accurate.
- Define abbreviations on first use, including RPM and CPM.

## Accessibility and privacy

- Use semantic HTML, sequential headings, descriptive links, accessible table headers, visible keyboard focus, and mobile-safe layouts.
- Never place sensitive personal data in URLs, analytics events, share text, or examples.
- Do not require an account, email address, or analytics consent to use an article, calculator, or download.

## Metadata and schema

Visible title, description, author, dates, breadcrumbs, and source claims must match metadata and JSON-LD. Add only schema types that describe content visibly present on the page and are supported by current search guidance.
