How Much Does YouTube Pay per 1,000 Views?

YouTube has no fixed payout per 1,000 views. For long-form ads, you can test a planning range of $0 to $12 per 1,000 total views, from earning nothing on those views to a higher ad revenue scenario. This is an illustrative range with no market ceiling implied. Your actual rate depends on your niche, where viewers watch, how long they watch and whether ads run. What is YouTube RPM, and how is it different from CPM?

RPM means revenue per thousand views. To get an ad-only rate, divide your estimated watch-page ad revenue by total long-form views for the same dates and multiply by 1,000. Include views that earned nothing. YouTube Studio’s overall RPM can also include Premium, memberships and Supers, so use the estimated ad revenue figure when you want ads alone.

CPM means cost per thousand ad impressions. It measures advertiser spend before revenue share, and its denominator is ad impressions. A view can have multiple ad impressions or none. Applying a platform split to CPM alone still leaves you with the wrong denominator for a per-view estimate.

How do I calculate what my YouTube views could earn?

The free worksheet divides monthly views by 1,000 and multiplies by the post-share ad revenue per thousand views you enter. Run the low and high cases separately. Keep your view count fixed across runs if you want to see how changing RPM alone affects the estimate.

For a hypothetical cooking channel, test 40,000 to 60,000 monthly long-form views and an ad-only RPM of $2 to $5. Pair the lower view count with the lower RPM and the higher view count with the higher RPM. Divide each view count by 1,000 and multiply by its paired rate to get an estimated $80 to $300 in monthly ad revenue before taxes or production costs. The endpoints reflect different assumptions about traffic, audience geography and watch time.

Which RPM should I use for my channel?

This method works best for monetized long-form creators budgeting with their own watch-page data. It is a poor fit for predicting Shorts income, sponsorship fees or earnings before ads are active. If you lack channel history, use the table to test sensitivity and keep zero in your planning range.

Start with recent periods that resemble your next month in topic, viewer geography and video format. Pull views and estimated ad revenue from the same dates and group similar videos together. Use lower and higher rates from comparable periods, and revisit the range if your audience or watch time changes.

Why did I earn less even though my views went up?

More views can come from videos or countries with fewer ad impressions per view or weaker advertiser demand. Compare total views and estimated ad revenue for the same content and dates, then recalculate the ad-only rate. That shows whether lower revenue per view offset the extra traffic.

What else do creators ask about YouTube pay?

Does YouTube pay for every view?

No. Total views include plays that generate no ad revenue, which is why the ad-only RPM calculation uses all views in the matching period. Views with no ads can pull down your rate even when overall traffic rises.

Can I use the same RPM for YouTube Shorts?

Use separate assumptions for Shorts and long-form videos. The watch-page calculator models long-form ads using an ad-only rate, so blending both formats can hide what each earns.

Should I subtract YouTube’s share after calculating RPM?

The worksheet expects creator ad revenue after revenue share. Use the post-share rate directly so you only account for the platform share once.
